Late Morning · Fondi

10:30

Dairy · Caseificio

Caseificio Paolella — the first buffalo mozzarella dairy in Fondi

Drive north on the SS7 or Via Flacca (see note below). Fondi is 20 minutes from Gaeta. Turn off to the Paolella dairy on Via Pantanello — a roadside caseificio, no ceremony. This family opened the first buffalo dairy in the Fondi area in 1933, bringing the tradition up from Campania when the drained Pontine marshes proved perfect for buffalo. Still family-run. Production happens in the morning so you arrive when everything is freshest.

What to buy: mozzarella di bufala (eat one immediately in the car park), ricotta di bufala (richer and more complex than cow's milk ricotta), burrata if they have it, and ask about the caciotta di bufala stagionata — a semi-aged buffalo cheese with nothing like it in a supermarket.

Mozzarella di Bufala DOP

Eat one on the spot. Freshness window is hours, not days. Buy extra to take to Rome.

Ricotta di Bufala

Denser, creamier, more mineral than cow's milk. Ask for it fresh. Keeps 2–3 days.

Caciotta di Bufala Stagionata

Semi-aged. Travels. Excellent with the coriander sausage you'll buy in an hour.

📍 Via Pantanello s.n., Fondi · +39 0771 511528 ⏰ Mornings only · Closes early afternoon 🦵 Roadside stop — flat, drive-in

Salumeria · Fondi

Coriander sausage — Salsiccia di Monte San Biagio

Back through Fondi town centre to any good macelleria (butcher). This sausage — PAT-protected, made only in this province — is pork seasoned with coriander, Moscato di Terracina wine, red pepper and chilli, smoked over mastic and myrtle wood. The coriander tradition comes from the Saracens, who used the spice to mask pork and make it permissible under Islamic dietary law. It is genuinely unlike any sausage you have eaten before.

Three forms available: fresh (eat tonight), dry-cured (travels to Rome and beyond), preserved in olive oil or lard (brings home). Buy all three if possible.

Salsiccia Fresca al Coriandolo

Fresh — cook or eat tonight. Ask the hotel restaurant if they'll prepare it for you.

Salsiccia Secca al Coriandolo

Dry-cured — takes the journey home. The format that travels.

Salsiccia Sott'Olio

Preserved in olive oil. Sealed jars travel internationally. Outstanding gift.

🏪 Any macelleria in Fondi centre — ask for "salsiccia al coriandolo"

Scenic Road · Mozzarella Shacks

Via Flacca north — roadside buffalo stops

From Fondi, take the Via Flacca (SS213) rather than the inland SS7. It's the coastal road, slower but far more beautiful — clifftop views of the Tyrrhenian, sea caves, and the approach to Terracina from the water side is spectacular.

Along this road between Fondi and Terracina you will pass informal roadside mozzarella shacks — small operations selling direct from farms, often a handwritten sign and a cooler. Stop at any that look active. No ceremony, no English required. Point at what you want. These are often even fresher than the caseificio and cost almost nothing.

🧀 Stop on impulse — no planning needed 🦵 Pull-off stops, entirely flat

Afternoon · Terracina

1:15
PM

Lunch · Lower Town

Terracina lower town — first look, then eat

Enter Terracina from the south, park in the lower town near the seafront (flat, easy). The lower town is built directly on Roman foundations — the original Via Appia stone cuts are visible in the cliff face along the approach; look for Roman numerals carved into the rock. Piazza del Municipio (the Aemilian Forum) is five minutes' walk and flat.

Lunch on the seafront — ask for the local specialty: pasta con le telline (tiny clams specific to this stretch of coast), or the frittura di paranza (fried mixed small fish from the morning catch). Simple fish restaurants all along the lungomare. Il Granchio is the most recommended local name; equally, walk until something looks right.

🐟 Telline are tiny; ask to confirm they're local, not imported 🦵 Lower town entirely flat — Roman grid layout
3:00

Drive · Optional Excursion

Temple of Jupiter Anxur — if the knees allow

Drive up (do not walk — it's a long climb). Park at the top near the archaeological zone. The temple is a 1st-century BC Republican sanctuary 210 metres above the city — from here you can see the entire Gulf of Gaeta to the south and the Circeo promontory to the north. On a clear June afternoon it is one of the finest views in Lazio.

The site itself is not extensively maintained — the structures are an honest ruin rather than a polished museum — but the vaulted substructures (cryptoporticus) are extraordinary and unlike anything in Rome. Budget 30–45 minutes. Entry around €10.

🚗 Drive up, don't walk — the road up is Via San Francesco Nuova 🦵 Uneven ground inside the site — take your time, no steps required ⏰ Open Tue–Sun, closes 7:30pm

Wine · Enoteca

Moscato di Terracina DOC — the local white in its hometown

Stop at any enoteca or good alimentari in the lower town and pick up a bottle of Moscato di Terracina. This is a dry-to-off-dry white made from a local cultivar of Moscato Bianco — nothing like the sweet Asti version. Coastal, mineral, slightly aromatic. You are in the only place it's made; it costs almost nothing here and you will not find it in Rome.

Moscato di Terracina DOC Secco or Abboccato

"Secco" is dry, "abboccato" is off-dry. Both work. Producers: Sant'Andrea or Cincinnato cooperative are the main names.

⊕ Optional Extension — adds ~1 hour to the day

The Itri Detour

From Fondi, instead of heading straight up the Via Flacca, you take a 15-minute inland loop through Itri before rejoining the coast road north of Sperlonga. Three specific things justify this detour that you cannot get anywhere else on the route.

Stop 1 · Itri valley — Frantoio

Colline Pontine DOP Olive Oil

The Itrana olive — the same cultivar as the Gaeta black olive you've been eating — produces a DOP-certified oil specific to this valley. It's intensely grassy, peppery, and vegetal in a way bottled oil never is. Look for a frantoio (mill) or cooperative selling direct on the road into Itri from Fondi — any local can point you to one. The oil is pressed from Itrana olives grown on the hillsides you can see from the road. Buy the largest bottle you can carry home.

⏱ 15 min stop 🦵 Valley road — flat

Stop 2 · Itri macelleria

Salamino al Coriandolo from Itri

Itri has its own tradition of coriander salami — slightly different from the Monte San Biagio sausage (different cure, smaller format). The Gambero Rosso specifically cited the Itri coriander salami as exceptional, paired with the local Viognier. A good macelleria in the lower part of Itri (avoid the hilltop unless your knees are excellent today) will have it alongside the broader range of salsiccia al coriandolo. If you only bought fresh sausage in Fondi, this is the place to add the dry-cured format for travel.

⏱ 20 min stop 🦵 Stay in the lower valley — do not climb the old town

Stop 3 · Monte San Biagio (optional add)

Marzolina Goat Cheese

Monte San Biagio is a small hill town 10 minutes from Itri. Marzolina is the only goat cheese made from the local herd here — set in traditional basket moulds, small and intense. It exists almost nowhere else, even in Rome. The best approach is to ask in any alimentari in the lower part of the town, or at the Itri macelleria (who may stock it). It's seasonal and small-production so it's not guaranteed — but if you find it, it's the rarest thing you'll bring home from this entire stretch.

⏱ 20 min if you find it quickly 🦵 Ask at lower town shops — don't climb unnecessarily

Rejoining the route: from Monte San Biagio or Itri, take the SP21 west toward the coast and pick up the Via Flacca north toward Terracina. You pass just above Sperlonga on this road — you'll see it below on the cliff and understand exactly why you made the right call not staying there. Total extension adds roughly 25km and 55–65 minutes to the drive, not counting stops.
