Main Event · Bacoli

Underwater Archaeological Park of Baia

🤿 What you're diving into

Baia was the greatest luxury resort of ancient Rome — emperors, senators, and poets kept villas here on the volcanic shoreline. Around the 4th–8th centuries AD, bradyseism (volcanic ground movement) slowly submerged the entire city beneath the bay. Today a Roman street grid, mosaic floors, statues, and the nymphaeum of Emperor Claudius lie 3–7 metres underwater. You'll swim through rooms that Caesar, Cicero, and Hadrian once walked.

Optional · Bacoli

Castello Aragonese di Baia — 30 min stop

If you have energy after diving, this clifftop Aragonese castle is a 5-minute drive and houses a remarkable museum of Roman artefacts from the submerged city — including original statues recovered from the bay. Open Tue/Thu–Sun 9am–5:30pm. Skip if you're tired and hungry — Gaeta is the better use of your afternoon.

💡 Open Tuesday, Thursday–Sunday only ✨ Originals of statues you'll have just swum past

Lunch · Bacoli / Pozzuoli

Seafood lunch on the water

The Bacoli waterfront and nearby Pozzuoli port have excellent, unpretentious seafood trattorias. Look for a place with tables outside near the marina and order spaghetti alle vongole or frittura di paranza (mixed fried seafood). You're in one of the most active fishing ports near Naples — the quality is exceptional and prices are local. Budget ~€15–20 per person.

Arrival · Gaeta

Your Base for Two Nights

Welcome to Gaeta

Gaeta is one of Lazio's great secrets — a medieval fortified peninsula jutting into its own crystal gulf, with Roman ruins on one side and a glittering beach on the other. Check in, drop the bags, and feel the shift from Naples' intensity to something much quieter.

After checking in, walk the Passeggiata di Gaeta Medievale — the old town is a single narrow promontory of lanes, arches, and stairways climbing between two seas. The light in late afternoon is extraordinary. Take the main coastal road into the medieval quarter and walk north toward the cathedral.

✨ Cathedral of Sant'Erasmo: split columns from an earthquake in 1231, said to have occurred at the moment of Christ's death ✨ Climb to any terrace for views of both sides of the peninsula simultaneously 💡 The old town is best explored on foot — car parks are outside

Sundowner · Gaeta

Aperitivo then dinner

Walk the lungomare for an Aperol Spritz as the sun drops behind Monte Orlando. Then choose your dinner — Gaeta has an outstanding local food scene for a town this size.

Bless Gaeta

Fine dining wine bar, hidden down Via dell'Indipendenza. Chef Francesca's rotating seasonal menu — the best cooking in town. Book ahead.

Osteria Il Ritrovo

Baccalà specialists — 12 preparations of salt cod. Tiny, beloved, lungomare location. Possibly the freshest fish in Gaeta.

Dolia

Contemporary Mediterranean in Piazza Conca, old town. Sleek modern interior, exceptional local ingredients. Great wine list.

Gaeta's signature dish: a sealed pastry filled with octopus, greens or escarole. Buy a slice from a bakery — not to miss.
