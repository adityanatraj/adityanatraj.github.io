Tuesday, June 2 · Your Day Guide

Gaeta

The peninsula city — sea on both sides, two thousand years of history in every stone

You arrived last night from the Baia dive and a long drive north. Today is the whole reason you routed through this stretch — Gaeta is one of the most undervisited medieval cities on the Italian coast. This is a day of slow, deliberate exploration. Knees are considered throughout: the old town has some slopes but nothing brutal, and the pace is yours to set.

Sanctuary · 20 min drive

Montagna Spaccata — the split mountain

Do this early before midday heat. Drive south from Le Rocce (15 min, free parking nearby on Via Santissima Trinità). The Sanctuary of the Most Holy Trinity is built into a dramatic crack in a coastal cliff — legend holds the rock split at the moment of the Crucifixion. There are two paths: one along the top of the gorge, one at the water's edge below. Take the top path (flatter, spectacular views). The lower path involves steps — skip if knees are unhappy today.

Knees: upper path only. It's a 10-min walk from the small parking area, no significant incline. The lower water-level path involves a long descent and return climb — save that for another lifetime.
✨ Free entry. Bring a small coin for the candle offering — it's tradition. 💡 La mano del Turco — look for the handprint in the rock face inside the grotto. Extraordinary.

Walk · Gaeta Vecchia

Gaeta Medievale — the old town

Park at the base of the old town on Lungomare Giovanni Caboto — easy flat street-level parking. From there you enter Gaeta Vecchia on foot. The Passeggiata di Gaeta Medievale is the main walking route; it's well-reviewed for a reason. Cathedral of Sant'Erasmo is along the way — modest entrance, extraordinary 13th-century candelabra inside. Wander without an agenda. This is a lived-in town, not a museum: laundry above alleys, men playing cards outside bars, cats on every windowsill.

Knees: the waterfront lungomare is flat and easy. The alleys climbing into the medieval core are cobbled and some have gentle slopes. Stay low and near the seafront if it's a harder day. The best views and character are right at the waterline anyway.
✨ Best detail: sea visible on both sides of the peninsula as you walk. You'll feel it before you see it. 💡 Fish market near the port opens early and sells out by late morning — just walk through and look

Lunch · Gaeta Vecchia

Eat the local thing — tiella and fresh fish

Your two best options for lunch. Go to whichever feels right when you're there — both are within the old town.

★★★★★ · 4.8 · Top Pick

Osteria Il Ritrovo

The baccalà (salt cod) restaurant — 12 preparations of cod. The cod carpaccio alone is worth the trip. Friendly, small, extremely fresh. Seafront location.

Closed Monday · Open Tue–Sun

★★★★½ · 4.6 · Great Choice

Marelì

In the old town. The ragù di totani (flying squid ragù) pasta is famous. Outdoor terrace, quick service, deeply local crowd. Go here for a trattoria feel.

Closed Wednesday

Tiella Gaetana

Don't leave Gaeta without eating this. A sealed pastry — short crust top and bottom — filled with octopus and local olives, or escarole, or salt cod. Buy a slice from a bakery (forno), not a restaurant. Eat it standing up or on a bench. This is the thing.

Olive di Gaeta (Itrana variety)

The local olive is something genuinely different — small, wrinkled, bitter-sweet, complex. Buy a container at any alimentari (grocery) or market stall. They don't travel far in commerce; this is one of the few places you can eat them truly fresh and cured locally.

💡 Italian lunch service is 12:30–2:30pm sharp. Arrive by 1pm to be safe.

Afternoon Rest · Optional Castle

Castello Angioino-Aragonese — or a quiet hour at Serapo

Two options depending on how your knees are feeling and how much you want to move vs. rest.

If you have energy: the Castello is extraordinary. Guided tours only (€10, €20 for English guide — worth every euro). A 13th-century castle-turned-prison that held political prisoners well into the 20th century. The views from the walls are some of the best on this coast. The tour is around an hour and is flat inside the fortifications. Guided tours leave at fixed times — check at the door.

If you'd rather rest: drive 5 minutes to Spiaggia di Serapo, rent a sun bed and umbrella (€15–20), swim in extraordinary blue Tyrrhenian water, and do absolutely nothing. This is equally valid and possibly smarter before tomorrow's drive to Sperlonga.

The castle involves some uneven cobbled surfaces inside. It's manageable but not totally flat. Serapo is sand-level — easier on the body. Your call.
🏖 Serapo water clarity is exceptional in June — still before peak crowds ✨ Castle: ask about the underground prison cells — the guide Stefano reportedly makes it special

Late Afternoon · Gelato

Evening walk — the seafront at golden hour

Gaeta's Lungomare at around 5:30–7pm, when the light goes golden and the temperature drops, is one of the nicest hours in this town. Walk the flat waterfront road, watch the boats, buy an ice cream from Gelateria Il Molo on the seafront. The old town is magical at this hour too — quieter than midday, lit by warm lanterns reflecting off the sea below.

✨ Il Molo gelato on the seafront: local recommendation, excellent pistacchio

Dinner · Old Town

Dolia — Cucina Mediterranea Contemporanea

This is the standout dinner choice. In Piazza Conca in the old town — the most beautiful and ambitious kitchen in Gaeta. Mediterranean contemporary, deep focus on local ingredients, every dish described as a "little work of art." The cod here is exceptional. If you want something extraordinary for your last night in Gaeta, this is it.

★★★★½ · 4.5 · Best in Gaeta

Dolia Gaeta

Piazza Conca, 22. Call ahead if possible: +39 0771 65129. Open: Tue/Thu–Sun dinner, Tue & Sun lunch. Closed Monday and Wednesday.

Or: Trattoria Donna Nina

If Dolia feels too formal or is full, Donna Nina is the warm neighborhood alternative — right on the water, great seafood pasta, owner serves personally. More relaxed, equally local.
